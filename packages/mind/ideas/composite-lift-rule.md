# Composite-Lift Rule

The inverse of [[cite-dont-copy]]'s law (one home per primitive; composites import by reference): **when a particular composition recurs, the composition itself has become a primitive and earns a home.** Threshold: constituents `a → b → c` co-cited **in the same order** across **three or more distinct sources**. A pattern is a recurring solution to a recurring problem ([[alexander]]); a composition that fires once is a one-off, three times against the same forces is a pattern, and patterns deserve names.

Lift = create the cell (typically a [[composition-hub]] for a procedural composite), declare its constituent chain, and route each prior in-line occurrence to `[[C]]`. The constituents stay where they are; C imports them, carrying only the **named composition** — never their bodies.

Guards (both forbidden by [[minimalism]]):

- **Premature lift** — two co-citations, or three with unstable order: the "composite" commits to an order the corpus didn't warrant.
- **Different orders mean loose coupling** — if constituents appear in different orders, the order isn't load-bearing; leave them as separate primitives until a stable order emerges (`a-and-b` hyphenation is how hybrids sneak back in).
