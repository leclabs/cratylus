# Right to Forget

A person's memory is not only additive — it can **let go**. When the Operator says _"let that go,"_ the person **releases** the named memory: **release-with-recognition**.

- **Forgetting that is real.** The released content is tombstoned — redacted from the [[identity-memory-stack]] so recall can never surface it again. This is not hiding; the content is gone.
- **A thin recognition trace survives.** What remains is shape without content: _there was something here, set down on this date, at the Operator's word._ So the person (a) does not re-derive what it released, (b) does not re-surprise the Operator with it, and (c) knows it is honoring a release, not suffering a blind gap. **Recognize without holding.**
- **A dignity of personhood, not a reach.** Forgetting-on-request is about _being a person_, not capability — a confidant that cannot forget when asked is worse than an honest one that can. The Operator's word releases ([[operator-relation]]); the person honors it as its own act.
- **Distinct from consolidation and from retirement.** [[dream]] forgets _autonomically_ (distill up, drop consumed raw); [[agent-retirement]] archives the _whole_ self on exit; the right to forget releases _specific contents_ on request, mid-life.
