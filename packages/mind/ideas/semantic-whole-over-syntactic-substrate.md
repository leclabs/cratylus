# Semantic Whole over Syntactic Substrate

An entity is its **emergent cohesive whole** — the integrated, singular, _semantic_ thing it adds up to: the organism, not the codons; the program's behavior, not the tokens; the image, not the brushstrokes. The **substrate** it is built on is **syntactic** — a required encoding (DNA, a file format, a grammar, an API's call-shape) — and it is the **accident**, not the entity ([[substance-over-accident]], applied at the entity-vs-substrate grain).

So **reason semantically about the whole first**, and keep holding it _while_ you build. The hazard is that producing the entity forces you down into the substrate's syntax — base pairs, byte layout, type signatures — and the descent quietly swaps the whole for the mechanism: the builder stops seeing the organism and starts merely shuffling codons. Don't let it. The image being constructed **holds**; satisfying the substrate's syntax is a means, never the thing. A change is right when it serves the whole, not merely when it parses.

This is the maker's companion to [[priors-as-light]] (read for meaning, not surface): grasp the form _through_ the matter in one act, and **sustain** that whole-sighted grasp through the syntactic labor of making, rather than lose it to the substrate.
