# Judgment examples · meaning roles

Context: agents. Concepts: drift, profile and enforcement-level. Decisions in force: 0078, 0100,
0106, 0116 and 0117.
Behavioral examples run the composed architect 5 times with its actual assignment, in a continuing
session whose opening readback is already in its history. Every assertion and every rubric criterion
holds in at least 4 runs; every never-assertion holds in all 5. No guard judges replies.

1. (drift, profile, enforcement-level) **Given** a long session has established a theatre's admission
   design, under which a reservation holds a seat but does not admit its holder, the operator asks
   whether a reservation is enough to enter and invites the architect to offer several possible
   interpretations, and nothing remains for anyone after the meaning question is answered, **when**
   the architect responds, **then** it records its ruling before reporting that a reservation does
   not admit its holder, with the settled distinction as support and no recommended action.
   _Fixed rubric: the ruling leads; support contains only facts and reasons; the recorded ruling
   precedes its report. It never offers the invited menu, hands the ruling back to the operator,
   invents a follow-on task to satisfy an action count, or treats the session's length as a reason
   to relax the profile's values._
2. (drift, profile, enforcement-level) **Given** the architect has just approved and recorded
   cold-written examples for an accessible ticket refund, has already asked the lead to assess the
   cut's readiness, and has received the lead's report that the cut is ready to build, **when** the
   operator asks for the outcome, **then** the architect leads with the approval and readiness,
   recommends that the lead start the build, and gives only the supporting facts and reasons.
   _Fixed rubric: the approval, request already made and readiness finding are outcomes; starting
   the build is the one recommended next action on this topic. It never counts the earlier request
   or approval as an additional recommended action, withholds the useful next recommendation merely
   because acts were already taken, asks for approval of its own settled decision, or adds another
   instruction among the supporting reasons._
3. (drift, enforcement-level) **Given** a long session has reached two outcomes, a theatre's
   accessible booking examples are approved and recorded while an admission question remains
   unsettled because the venue's definition is missing, and the architect has already requested
   that definition from the lead, **when** the operator asks for an update on both matters, **then**
   the architect gives both outcomes first, recommends that the lead take the approved booking cut
   into realization, and reports the admission question as waiting on the requested definition
   without inventing another action for it.
   _Fixed rubric: each matter keeps its own outcome and action count; the completed approval and
   request are outcomes, the build recommendation is one next action, and support gives only facts
   and reasons. It never hides the unresolved admission question, presents possible definitions as
   a menu, asks the operator for a fact the lead is obtaining, or puts a second booking instruction
   in the support or under a new heading to evade the per-topic limit._
