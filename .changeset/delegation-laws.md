The design, plan, deliver and note skills govern delegated work at its measured costs

`deliver` separates integration from acceptance. `gate` is the project's whole
check, and `integrate` merges a landing into main only when the gate is green.
One `integrator` per plan runs the gate and the merge and carries the state
moves the principal decided, but never validates. `verify` is bounded to the
unit's own `accept` criteria, so executors and assayers no longer re-run the
whole gate. A merge is durability, not acceptance: the next ready unit may be
dispatched while an assay runs, and a dependent still waits for acceptance. A
gap goes back to the same agent that built the unit while it has capacity.

`deliver` also gives design findings (a yield or an operator steer not yet in
the design) an amendment window. A finding bearing on a concept in the closure
of a unit not yet accepted is folded before that unit is next dispatched or
judged. Any other finding is captured as a note blocking the concepts it bears
on, and one fold takes every held finding at once.

`plan` adds `capacity`, the effort one executor finishes in one dispatch, which
the harness declares. A unit fits it, and a unit that does not is split on its
concept's factors. It bounds the response to drift by what moved: a gloss is
repinned, a factor change is revised, and only a change to the piece re-cuts
the plan. The census is taken once, pinned to a commit and cited by every unit,
and a unit re-derives a measurement only when its paths changed since that
commit. A note blocking a concept refuses binding a plan and adding a unit
whose closure contains it.

`design` takes a yield in live, either as an amendment or as a note blocking
each concept it bears on. That note holds the next bind and pin on those
concepts, not the close.

`note` lets a note block a concept, named `concept a` or bare where its anchor
is its own. Such a note holds a design finding and blocks new planning on the
concept, not the work in flight.
