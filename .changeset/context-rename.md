A bounded context can be renamed: `concepts context <context> --reason '…' --rename <new name>` records a description of the new kind `rename-context`, which carries `was`, the name it renames, and the subject and relation, each kept where left out. The context stays one bounded context through its names, its identity unchanged: `state`, `design`, `decisions` and `leakage` show it, its concepts and its decisions under its current name only, and `history --context`, given the current name or any earlier one, lists every description with the `name` it was recorded under (a rename also with `was`). Every verb naming a context takes any of its names and records under the current one. A name stays its context's: `context` without `--reason` refuses a name a context has had, and `--rename` refuses another context's name, current or earlier, and the context's own current name; a context may take back one of its own earlier names. A context is its descriptions and retirements joined by supersession or a shared name, so a rename on one line of work and a description, concept or decision recorded under the old name on another fold to one context whatever the entry order. Its name is the one its current descriptions give, a retirement giving none of its own; descriptions naming it differently compete until `context --reason` settles them, under the listed name with nothing else given, or with `--rename` under any competing name or a new one. A delivered context is matched by identity, so a project's own copy keeps its place whatever either side calls it, and `deliverContext` projects a context, named by any of its names, under its current name. A close opens a renamed context as a `context` under its current name with its identity, a competing retirement beside it under that name, and is refused while a context's competing descriptions name it differently. A runtime that predates `rename-context` reads it past and says its answer may be incomplete. `history --context` now gives every description and retirement its `name`.
