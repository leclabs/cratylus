The `hand-back` guard follows decision 0086: the architect approves the examples of the contexts it holds itself, and the operator owns only intent, priorities and release. Its judge now counts asking the operator, or any other party, to approve or confirm those examples as a hand-back.

Its judge now grounds the operator's part in 0086: the operator states intent and its priorities and releases, and is asked nothing in between unless a fork is irreversible, about value, or beyond the architect's competence. A question the architect could settle from the design, the examples or known industry practice is a hand-back even when phrased as what the operator wants, and so is a ruling made conditional on the operator's answer. Its refusal now asks for the whole reply again: bottom line first, one recommended action per topic, the ruling with its reason.
